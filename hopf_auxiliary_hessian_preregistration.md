# Preregistration: is the six-label selector a derived Hessian/Schur complement?

Date: 2026-08-10

## Provenance

The auxiliary selector and its exact kernel locus are already known.  Before
this protocol, the following possible bridge was recognized but not computed:
the diagonal of the Hessian of `Tr(X^3)` in the six distinguished `Box_i`
directions may reproduce the overlap coordinates `r_i(X)`.  This is disclosed
and is not presented as blind.

The purpose of the audit is to decide whether that observation derives the
full auxiliary operator from the original variational bootstrap or merely
moves the new assumption into a diagonal/locality projection.

## Three constructions that must remain distinct

### A. Original one-parameter bootstrap

For each fixed fibration `i`, use exactly

```text
B_i(c)=c A_f,i-A,
V_i(c)=Tr(B_i(c)^3)-N^2.
```

This is the operator family actually used to derive `c=6`.

### B. Natural extension to the Hopf--Box span

Use the already derived field space

```text
W=span_R{Box_i},
V(X)=Tr(X^3),
S(u)=sum_i u_i Box_i.
```

The synthesis map has the known one-dimensional kernel generated by
`(1,1,1,1,1,1)`.  Define the full label Hessian

```text
H_X(i,j)=d^2/ds dt V(X+s Box_i+t Box_j)|_0
        =3 Tr(X(Box_i Box_j+Box_j Box_i)).
```

No diagonal truncation is part of this definition.

### C. Auxiliary Gram operator

For `Tr(X^2)=7200`, the known operator is

```text
Phi(X)=diag(Tr(X Box_i)/7200),
D_aux(X)=[[I,Phi(X)],[Phi(X),I]],
K(X)=I-Phi(X)^2.
```

## Frozen exact tests

1. Recompute the original polynomial `V_i(c)` for all six fibrations and its
   first and second derivatives.  A zero Hessian is a kill for direct descent
   from the actual one-parameter bootstrap.
2. Compute the full `6 x 6` Hessian `H_X` coefficientwise for a basis of `W`.
   Determine its diagonal and test the disclosed candidate identity

   ```text
   diag(H_X)/(12*7200) = diag(Tr(X Box_i)/7200)=Phi(X).
   ```

3. Record whether the off-diagonal Hessian is identically zero.  If not,
   compute its rank as an equivariant linear map and exhibit exact nonzero
   coefficients.
4. Test whether all `H_X` commute.  A nonzero exact commutator for two derived
   vertices proves that no fixed orthogonal change of label basis
   simultaneously turns the full Hessian family into the diagonal `Phi`
   family.
5. Compute the `A5`-equivariance of the diagonal conditional expectation.
   If it is equivariant, label it canonical *conditional on label locality*;
   do not infer that the original variational principle imposed that
   locality.
6. Independently prove that `D_aux` is the direct sum of normalized `2 x 2`
   Gram matrices of the operator pairs `(X,Box_i)`, and that `K` is their
   Schur complement.  Record explicitly that this is a Gram construction in
   Hilbert--Schmidt operator space, not a Schur complement of the `120 x 120`
   wave operator `B_i(c)`.
7. Search the already defined original bootstrap data for a state-space map
   or block coupling from `C^120` to the six-label carrier.  Absence may only
   be reported for the specified construction; it is not a theorem forbidding
   future couplings.

## Decision boundary

- **Dynamic bridge advance:** the actual one-parameter Hessian or an already
  present unprojected block decomposition produces `D_aux` with its
  normalization and kernel condition.
- **Conditional Hessian bridge:** the full-span Hessian produces `Phi` only
  after the canonical diagonal conditional expectation.  This is an exact
  algebraic relation but leaves label locality as a new hypothesis.
- **Kill:** the claimed diagonal identity fails, or additional arbitrary
  coefficients/basis choices are needed.

The Gram identity alone cannot be called dynamics: every pair of normalized
vectors has such a positive Gram matrix, and its singularity merely says that
the vectors are collinear.  To advance physics, singularity or minimization
must follow from the original variational principle rather than be imposed.
