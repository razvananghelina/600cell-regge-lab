## The Idea: Higgs VEV Profile on the 600-Cell

In the standard NCG spectral action approach, fermion masses come from the Yukawa term:
$$\langle \psi, D_F \Phi \psi \rangle$$

where Φ is the Higgs field and D_F is the finite Dirac operator. When the Higgs acquires a VEV, the fermion mass matrix becomes:

$$M_f = Y_f \cdot v$$

where Y_f is the Yukawa coupling and v = 246 GeV is the Higgs VEV.

**In our framework**, the 600-cell replaces continuous spacetime. The Higgs field is a function Φ: V₆₀₀ → ℂ on the 120 vertices of the 600-cell. The key question is: **what is the VEV profile?**

If the VEV is **uniform** (Φ = v at all vertices), then all fermions of the same type get the same coupling, and there's no generation-dependent correction. This is the "bare" formula.

But if the VEV has a **non-uniform profile** determined by minimizing the Higgs potential on the 600-cell graph, then:
- Fermion wave functions at different vertices see different local VEVs
- The effective Yukawa coupling depends on the overlap integral ∫ψ†·Φ·ψ
- Different generations (which correspond to different harmonic modes on the 600-cell) get different corrections

This could derive the deltas from pure geometry with **zero free parameters**.

### Part 3: Higgs Potential and VEV Profile on the 600-Cell

The Higgs potential on the 600-cell graph is:
$$V[\Phi] = \sum_{v} \left[ -\mu^2 |\Phi(v)|^2 + \lambda |\Phi(v)|^4 \right] + \frac{\kappa}{2} \sum_{\langle v,w \rangle} |\Phi(v) - \Phi(w)|^2$$

The first sum is the Mexican hat potential at each vertex. The second sum is the **discrete gradient energy** (kinetic term on the graph), where the sum runs over the 720 edges.

The parameters are:
- μ² > 0 (drives symmetry breaking)
- λ > 0 (stabilizes the potential)  
- κ > 0 (stiffness / gradient penalty)

**Key insight:** The ratio κ/μ² determines the VEV profile:
- **κ/μ² → ∞**: VEV is uniform (constant on all vertices) → bare formula, no corrections
- **κ/μ² → 0**: VEV is independent at each vertex (random) → maximal corrections
- **Intermediate κ/μ²**: VEV has a non-trivial profile determined by geometry

**Minimize V[Φ]** for the 600-cell graph:

1. **Uniform solution:** Φ(v) = v₀ = μ/√(2λ) for all v. This is always a critical point. Check its stability (compute the Hessian).

2. **Non-uniform solutions:** Decompose Φ in terms of graph Laplacian eigenmodes:
$$\Phi(v) = v_0 + \sum_k a_k \, e_k(v)$$
where e_k are eigenvectors of L. Substitute into V and minimize over the coefficients a_k.

3. **The physical question:** For what range of κ/μ² does the non-uniform VEV exist? What is its profile? Specifically, **which Laplacian eigenmodes get activated** in the VEV?

**Expected result:** The first non-uniform instability should occur when the lowest non-zero Laplacian eigenvalue λ₁ ≈ 1.53 (the ρ₂ mode) satisfies:
$$\kappa \lambda_1 < 2\mu^2$$
In this regime, the VEV develops a ρ₂-shaped modulation on top of the uniform background.

### Part 4: Effective Yukawa Couplings

With a non-uniform VEV Φ(v), the effective Yukawa coupling for fermion f is:

$$y_f^{\text{eff}} = \frac{1}{|\text{2I}|} \sum_{v \in \text{600-cell}} \psi_f^*(v) \cdot \Phi(v) \cdot \psi_f(v)$$

This is the **overlap integral** of the fermion wave function with the VEV profile.

For a uniform VEV, y_f^eff = v₀ for all f (no correction).

For a VEV with profile Φ(v) = v₀ + εΦ₁(v) where Φ₁ is a Laplacian eigenmode:

$$y_f^{\text{eff}} = v_0 + \epsilon \cdot \frac{1}{|2I|} \sum_v |\psi_f(v)|^2 \cdot \Phi_1(v)$$

The correction depends on the **triple product** of the fermion density |ψ_f|² with the VEV mode Φ₁. By representation theory, this is non-zero only when the tensor product of the fermion irrep with itself contains the VEV irrep.

**Compute these overlaps** for all 9 fermion types and determine the effective mass correction:

$$\delta_f = \frac{y_f^{\text{eff}} - v_0}{v_0 \cdot \ln\varphi}$$

(dividing by ln φ to convert from fractional mass change to exponent shift)

- The Laplacian eigenvectors group into irrep blocks of size dₖ²

- The overlap integrals reduce to **Clebsch-Gordan coefficients** of 2I

So the full computation might reduce to:
$$\delta_f \propto \sum_k \epsilon_k \cdot \text{CG}(ρ_f \otimes \bar{ρ}_f, ρ_k) \cdot \text{(amplitude of mode k in VEV)}$$

This is a tractable algebraic computation once you have the CG coefficients.
