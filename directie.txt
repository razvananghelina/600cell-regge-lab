TASK: Derive continuous gauge groups from discrete 2I symmetry via Zariski closure

CONTEXT:
I'm developing a theoretical physics framework ("One Integer, Three Generations") where the Standard Model gauge group SU(3)×SU(2)×U(1) emerges from the binary icosahedral group 2I (order 120), which is the symmetry group of the 600-cell polytope.

The framework derives a representation decomposition from A5 ≅ 2I/Z2 acting on the 12-vertex icosahedron (vertex figure of the 600-cell):

  12 = 1 ⊕ 3 ⊕ 3' ⊕ 5

where 3 and 3' are the two inequivalent 3-dimensional irreps of A5, distinguished by the Galois automorphism φ ↔ φ' (their characters on 5-cycles are φ and φ' respectively). The gauge group assignment is:

  12 = 1(U(1)) + 3'(SU(2) adjoint, dim 3) + 3⊕5(SU(3) adjoint, dim 8)

This decomposition is proven unique (only valid adjoint partition).

THE CRITICAL GAP:
The main criticism of this framework is: "Finite groups cannot generate continuous gauge symmetries." A5 has 60 elements; SU(3) is a continuous 8-dimensional Lie group. How does one get from discrete to continuous?

WHAT I NEED YOU TO PROVE:
The bridge is the ZARISKI CLOSURE. Specifically, I need rigorous proofs of:

1. MAIN THEOREM: The Zariski closure of Im(2I → GL₃(ℂ)) via the 3-irrep ρ₃ is SL₃(ℂ).

   Expected proof strategy:
   a) The 3-irrep ρ₃: 2I → GL₃(ℂ) is irreducible (this is standard — verify).
   b) The image ρ₃(2I) is NOT contained in any proper algebraic subgroup of SL₃(ℂ).
      This requires showing it's not in any parabolic, not in any normalizer of a torus, 
      not in any finite extension of such.
   c) By a theorem of algebraic groups (possibly Borel density or a variant), 
      an irreducible finite subgroup of SL_n whose Zariski closure is connected 
      and reductive must have Zariski closure = SL_n.
   d) Alternatively, use the Larsen-Pink theorem or the classification of maximal 
      finite subgroups of SL₃(ℂ) to establish this.

2. COROLLARY FOR SU(2): The Zariski closure of Im(2I → GL₃(ℂ)) via the 3'-irrep ρ₃' is also SL₃(ℂ), but since dim(3') = 3 = dim(adjoint of SU(2)) + 0, and 3' decomposes under a specific SU(2) ⊂ SL₃ as the adjoint, we need:
   
   The restriction of ρ₃' to the appropriate subgroup gives the SU(2) adjoint.
   Specifically: 3' ≅ ad(SU(2)) as an A5-module.

3. THE FULL GAUGE GROUP: Combine the above to show:
   
   The Zariski closures of the three A5-irrep factors in the decomposition 
   12 = 1 ⊕ 3 ⊕ 3' ⊕ 5 generate exactly U(1) × SU(2) × SU(3):
   
   - 1-irrep → U(1) (abelian, trivial)
   - 3'-irrep → SU(2) (via adjoint identification)
   - 3⊕5 → SU(3) (via adjoint decomposition ad(SU(3))|_{A5} = 3 ⊕ 5)

4. GALOIS SELECTION: The physical choice φ > 0 (rather than φ' > 0) breaks the 
   discrete Galois symmetry 3 ↔ 3', selecting which irrep gives SU(2) and which 
   contributes to SU(3). Prove this is the ONLY ambiguity and that the Galois 
   automorphism σ: √5 → -√5 is the unique symmetry swapping the two assignments.
