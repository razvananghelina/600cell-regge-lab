# Preregistration: minimal first-class conversion of Whitney copy constraints

Date: 2026-08-11

## Fixed second-class input

Let (M=M_{\rm loc}>0), (A=A_{\rm loc}=A^*), and let (C) have independent
rows spanning the canonical neighbour-constraint row space.  The copy
constraints ​(phi=Cu) have bracket Gram

\[
G=CM^{-1}C^*>0

\]

on the independent row space and are second class by commit `982aa44`.

Test the minimal linear Batalin--Fradkin/Stueckelberg-type conversion.  No
claim is made that this formalism is selected by the 600-cell; the purpose is
to determine whether even the minimal conversion removes or merely relocates
the nonlocal inverse.

## Frozen auxiliary algebra

Add one complex auxiliary coordinate ​(eta\in\mathbb C^r) per independent
complex constraint, with bracket

\[
\{\eta,\eta^*\}=+iG,

\]

opposite to

\[
\{Cu,(Cu)^*\}=-iG.

\]

Define

\[
\Phi=Cu+\eta.

\]

The complete first-class gate is the exact identity

\[
\{\Phi,\Phi^*\}=0.

\]

The extended complex carrier has dimension (n+r); (r) complex first-class
constraints remove (2r) complex phase-space directions and leave physical
dimension (n-r), equal to the assembled cochains.

## Unique gauge-invariant dressing

Freeze a linear dressed coordinate

\[
\widetilde u=u-X\eta

\]

which equals (u) on the gauge slice ​(eta=0).  Gauge invariance under
(Phi) requires

\[
GX^*=CM^{-1}.

\]

Since (G>0), the solution is unique:

\[
X^*=G^{-1}CM^{-1},
\qquad
X=M^{-1}C^*G^{-1}.

\]

Sign conventions for the definition of ​(widetilde u) and the auxiliary
bracket must be checked consistently; changing both signs does not change the
support or uniqueness result.

On the first-class surface ​(eta=-Cu), the dressed coordinate must reduce to
the Dirac projector

\[
\widetilde u=
\left(I-M^{-1}C^*G^{-1}C\right)u=P_Du.

\]

Thus any exact local-bypass claim must defeat the appearance of (G^{-1}),
not merely write first-class constraints.

## Quadratic Hamiltonian gate

Consider the most general Hermitian quadratic extension whose restriction at
(eta=0) has the fixed physical block (A):

\[
H_{\rm ext}=
\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B^*&D\end{pmatrix}.

\]

Gauge invariance under the translation generated by ​(Phi) gives linear
block equations.  In particular the top equation fixes

\[
BG=AM^{-1}C^*,

\]

hence uniquely

\[
B=AM^{-1}C^*G^{-1}.

\]

The remaining equation fixes the action of (D) on the gauge directions.
Record the complete solution and verify that the gauge generator lies in the
kernel of the extended quadratic Hessian.

## Independent exact control

On the boundary of a 4-simplex, use the same exact rational matrices

\[
n=75,qquad r=45.

\]

Verify exactly:

1. the first-class bracket cancellation;
2. the unique dressing equation;
3. reduction to the exact Dirac projector;
4. the fixed top Hamiltonian equation;
5. the complete gauge-kernel equation for (H_{\rm ext}).

Count exact nonzeros of (X), (B), and the Dirac projector connecting
different tetrahedron blocks.  This support count is a control, not a
full-complex theorem.

## Decision boundary

- **DERIVED LOCAL FIRST-CLASS BYPASS:** the converted constraints and a
  gauge-invariant Hamiltonian are both block/neighbour local without an
  inverse or cross-star support.
- **DERIVED RELOCATION NO-GO:** the constraint algebra becomes first class,
  but the unique dressing/Hamiltonian contains (G^{-1}) and has exact
  cross-tetrahedron support.
- **REFUTED:** bracket cancellation, uniqueness or the projector reduction
  fails.

The negative label closes only this minimal linear conversion with fixed
physical block (A).  Nonlinear conversions, extra towers, a Krein/BRST
construction or a different physical Hamiltonian remain open, but their new
structure must be geometrically selected.

Only the new targeted verifier will be run.
