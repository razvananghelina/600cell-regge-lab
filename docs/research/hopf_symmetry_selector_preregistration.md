# Preregistration: does 600-cell symmetry select a Hopf fibration?

Date: 2026-08-10

## Question and scope

The preceding refinement tests establish a convergent smooth Hopf split after
one order-ten cyclic subgroup has been chosen.  They do **not** establish that
the 600-cell chooses that subgroup.  This test asks whether the complete
finite family of order-ten-subgroup Hopf fibrations selects a rank-one
vertical projector, or whether symmetry averages the family to the round
tangent metric.

The scope is deliberately limited to Hopf fibrations whose discrete fibres
are cosets of an order-ten cyclic subgroup of the binary icosahedral vertex
group.  It does not classify arbitrary smooth fibrations of `S^3`.

## Framing correction fixed before computation

The existing enumeration in
`reproducible/verify_whitney_hopf_blind_enumeration.py` constructs fibres
`qH`.  This is one chiral class.  The mirror construction has fibres `Hq` and
was not enumerated by that routine.  The new test must enumerate both; it may
not call the old list "all fibrations" without the qualifier "in one chiral
coset class".

Quaternion conjugation is the proposed mirror: it sends `qH` to
`conj(H) conj(q)`.  Whether this maps the two computed partition families
bijectively will be checked exactly rather than assumed.

## Exact construction, frozen before output is inspected

1. Implement the quadratic field `Q(sqrt(5))` as rational pairs `(a,b)` for
   `a+b*sqrt(5)`.  No floating tolerance is used for the load-bearing tests.
2. Rebuild the standard 120 unit quaternions of `2I` from the three coordinate
   families used by `commons/cell600.py`.  Verify exact norms, closure,
   inverses and identity.
3. Compute the order of every element by exact quaternion multiplication.
   Deduplicate the cyclic subgroups generated by all order-ten elements.
4. For every such subgroup `H`, compute the exact set partitions into left
   cosets `qH` and right cosets `Hq`.  Record the number of distinct partitions
   in each handed family and their overlap.  No equality or disjointness of
   the two families is assumed by the protocol.
5. For each `H`, take the imaginary line of any order-ten generator.  The sign
   and the choice of generator are removed by forming the exact rank-one
   projector

   `P_H = u u^T / (u^T u)`.

   Verify that every generator of the same subgroup gives the same projector.
6. Compute the exact conjugation action of `2I` on the subgroup/projector
   family.  Record its orbit decomposition and solve the coefficient fixed
   space for linear combinations `sum_H c_H P_H`.
7. Compute, exactly, the projector Gram matrix and frame operator

   `F = sum_H P_H`.

   Report all entries.  Isotropy means that the traceless part
   `F-Tr(F) I_3/3` is exactly zero; it is not a tolerance test.
8. Lift the identity to every `q in S^3` using the two tangent fields
   `X_H^R(q)=q u_H` and `X_H^L(q)=u_H q`.  This step is algebraic: unit
   quaternion multiplication is orthogonal and maps the imaginary subspace
   onto `T_q S^3`.  Therefore an exact scalar identity at the identity lifts
   to the corresponding tangent-projector identity everywhere.

## Falsifiers and permitted conclusions

- If the exact group construction fails, no selector conclusion is allowed.
- If conjugation has more than one orbit, equal weighting is not forced across
  the whole family; each orbit is a separate canonical coefficient.
- If the invariant coefficient space has dimension greater than one, symmetry
  leaves an anisotropy parameter and does not uniquely select the average.
- If the frame operator has a nonzero traceless part, the family itself
  supplies a canonical anisotropic tensor.  This would be a genuine positive
  selector result.
- If the action is transitive, the invariant coefficient line is generated by
  equal weights, and the exact frame operator is scalar, then **DERIVED
  NEGATIVE**: full unbroken 600-cell symmetry selects no individual Hopf
  fibration and no quadratic anisotropy within the linear span of these
  projectors.
- In that negative case, choosing one fibration remains possible only after
  adding a symmetry-breaking order parameter, boundary condition or dynamics.
  The existence and physical interpretation of such a mechanism remain
  **OPEN**.  This test must not call symmetry breaking derived.

For a kinetic tensor `K_H=P_T+(r-1)P_H`, an isotropic equal-weight average may
change only the overall scalar coefficient.  It cannot select `r`, a speed,
Lorentzian signature or the old integer `a1=5`.  Those are comparisons made
only after the exact enumeration is committed.

## Provenance status

This file freezes the algorithm, scope and falsifiers before the exact
enumeration verifier is written or run.  The analytic possibility of an
isotropic tight frame has already been stated in discussion, so this is not a
claim of a blind prediction.  The purpose of the commit ordering is narrower:
to prevent changing the enumeration or its acceptance boundary after seeing
the exact output.
