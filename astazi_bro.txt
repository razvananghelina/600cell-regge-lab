# EXPERIMENT: Lie Bracket Verification on the 12 Gauge Modes

## Context

From exp100-101, we have:
- ker(□_edge) = 13 = 1 + 12 on the line graph of the 600-cell
- The 12 gauge modes live on ρ₅ (branching node of E₈)
- The fiber graph (12 nodes, degree 5) decomposes under A₅ as 1 + 3 + 3' + 5
- We CLAIM: 12 = 1 + 3 + (3'+5) = u(1) + su(2) + su(3)

## What needs verification

The dimensions 1 + 3 + 8 match, but do the 12 modes actually carry the Lie algebra structure? Specifically:

### Test 1: Construct the 12 gauge mode vectors

Extract the 13 kernel vectors of □_edge (720-dimensional each). Separate:
- 1 vector in ρ₀ (the trivial mode)
- 12 vectors in 2·ρ₅ (the gauge modes)

### Test 2: Define a bracket operation

On edge space, there's a natural antisymmetric product. For two edge modes f, g ∈ R^720, define the bracket at each vertex v:

[f, g](e) = Σ_{triangles containing e} ε(e, e', e'') · f(e') · g(e'')

where the sum runs over all triangles containing edge e, and ε is the orientation sign. This is the discrete exterior product (wedge product on 1-forms), projected back to 1-forms via Hodge duality.

Alternatively, use the simpler vertex-mediated bracket:

[f, g]_v = Σ_{e1, e2 incident to v} ε(e1, e2) · f(e1) · g(e2)

where ε encodes orientation (±1 based on edge direction relative to v).

### Test 3: Check closure

Do the 12 gauge modes close under the bracket? 
- [gauge, gauge] should give gauge (not modes outside ker)
- The structure constants should match su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)

### Test 4: Decompose into subalgebras

Using the A₅ decomposition from the fiber graph:
- The 1-dimensional subspace (eigenvalue 0): should commute with everything → u(1)
- The 3-dimensional subspace (eigenvalue 5-√5): should close under bracket → su(2)
- The 8-dimensional subspace (eigenvalue 5+√5 and 6, i.e., 3'+5): should close under bracket → su(3)

Cross-brackets:
- [u(1), su(2)] = 0 (commutes)
- [u(1), su(3)] = 0 (commutes)  
- [su(2), su(3)] = 0 (commutes, since they're independent factors)

### Test 5: Structure constants

For su(2): [T_a, T_b] = ε_abc T_c (3 generators, structure constants = Levi-Civita)
For su(3): [T_a, T_b] = i f_abc T_c (8 generators, structure constants = Gell-Mann f_abc)

Compute the structure constants from the bracket and compare.

### Test 6: Killing form

The Killing form K(X,Y) = Tr(ad_X · ad_Y) should be:
- Negative definite on su(2) and su(3) (compact)
- Zero on u(1)
- Block diagonal (no mixing between factors)

## Alternative approach: Use the fiber graph directly

Since the 12 gauge modes are 100% on fiber edges, and the fiber graph has 12 nodes with A₅ decomposition 1+3+3'+5, we can work entirely on the 12-dimensional fiber graph.

The adjacency matrix of the fiber graph (12×12) is known. Its eigenspaces give the 1+3+3'+5 decomposition. 

Define the Lie bracket on the fiber graph: if the 12 fibers are labeled 0-11, and the adjacency encodes which fibers are "connected" (share cross edges), then:

[e_i, e_j] = Σ_k c_ijk e_k

where c_ijk comes from the triangle structure of the fiber graph (triangles = 3 mutually connected fibers).

Count triangles in the fiber graph. If the fiber graph has the right triangle structure, the bracket might naturally produce su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1).

## Alternative approach: McKay-based bracket

The 2I irrep ρ₅ has dimension 6. The tensor product ρ₅ ⊗ ρ₅ can be decomposed into symmetric and antisymmetric parts. The antisymmetric part Λ²(ρ₅) gives the Lie algebra structure.

Compute: Λ²(ρ₅) = ? (using 2I character table)
dim(Λ²(ρ₅)) = 6×5/2 = 15

Does Λ²(ρ₅) contain ρ₅ itself? If yes, then ρ₅ carries a Lie bracket.

The decomposition of Λ²(ρ₅) into 2I irreps will tell us the structure.

For reference, the 2I irreps are:
ρ₀(1), ρ₁(2), ρ₂(3), ρ₃(4), ρ₄(5), ρ₅(6), ρ₆(3), ρ₇(4), ρ₈(2)

We need 2·ρ₅ in the gauge sector. So the bracket on 2·ρ₅ involves:
Λ²(2·ρ₅) = Λ²(ρ₅) ⊕ Λ²(ρ₅) ⊕ (ρ₅ ⊗ ρ₅)

Actually: for 2·ρ₅, the antisymmetric square is:
Λ²(ρ₅ ⊕ ρ₅) = Λ²(ρ₅) ⊕ (ρ₅ ⊗ ρ₅) ⊕ Λ²(ρ₅)

Hmm, this is getting complicated. Let me simplify.

The SIMPLEST test: compute the 12×12×12 structure constant tensor c_ijk from the bracket of the 12 kernel vectors, and check if the resulting algebra is isomorphic to su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1).

An algebra is determined (up to isomorphism) by:
- Dimension: 12 ✓
- Rank: 1 + 1 + 2 = 4 (number of Cartan generators)
- Killing form signature
- Cartan matrix

## Important notes

1. The bracket might require normalization. The kernel vectors from numerical eigendecomposition are orthonormal in R^720, but the Lie bracket might need a different normalization.

2. If the bracket doesn't close exactly on the 12 modes (some leakage to massive modes), measure the leakage. Small leakage might indicate that the Lie algebra is approximate (broken by lattice effects).

3. The u(1) mode (ρ₀, dim 1) should be CENTRAL — it commutes with everything. This is the easiest test and should be done first.

4. If the bracket test fails completely, this is a valid negative result. The 12 modes would still have the right dimensions but not the right algebraic structure — reducing the result from "gauge algebra derived" to "gauge dimensions derived."

## Verification order

1. First: check if ρ₀ mode commutes with all 12 gauge modes (u(1) centrality)
2. Second: check if 3-dim subspace (eigenvalue 5-√5) closes under bracket
3. Third: check if 8-dim subspace (3'+5) closes under bracket
4. Fourth: compute full structure constants and compare with su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1)
5. Fifth: Killing form

## Blind procedure

Compute bracket → check closure → extract structure constants → THEN compare with known Lie algebras. Do not assume su(3) ⊕ su(2) ⊕ u(1) and fit to it.
